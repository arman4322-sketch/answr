# Answr — First-Batch Outreach Drafts

Ready-to-send outreach for the demand-validation sprint (see GROWTH.md), one set per buyer
segment. Each was drafted and then adversarially critiqued for reply rate + deliverability.
Swap `{{first}}` / `{{company}}` for real details and **personalize the first line** — that's
the biggest lever. Waitlist URL: https://useanswr.com/waitlist

> **Before you send cold email at volume:** add a CAN-SPAM footer (real mailing address +
> working opt-out), authenticate SPF/DKIM/DMARC, send from a *warmed subdomain* (not the root
> domain), keep first batches small, and segment out EU/UK contacts (GDPR/PECR). For the very
> first touch, consider dropping the link and just asking for a reply — send the link to repliers.

**Personalization tips:** Speak their language: they think in organic sessions, SERP rankings, and content ROI — frame AI answers as the new zero-click threat to traffic they already earned (in-house angle = "protect what you built," never "get more clients"). Biggest reply-rate lever is the personalized first line — swap the generic opener for a real signal and merge it into the hook, e.g. "Your 'best [category] tools' guide ranks well in Google — but does ChatGPT cite it when a buyer asks for a tool like {{company}}?" or name a category term they want to win, or a competitor they'd hate to see recommended over them. Naming a plausible buyer question ("best [category] tool for [use case]") makes the pain concrete. Keep the founder-to-practitioner, reality-check tone — you're asking for an honest opinion, not booking a demo; the link is deliberately demoted to a bottom parenthetical so the email reads as a conversation, not a funnel. (curiosity), "does ChatGPT recommend {{company}}, or a competitor?" (sharper sting, slightly longer), "is {{company}} showing up in AI answers?" (softer). Test reply rate, not just opens.

**Deliverability / spam risk:** Content/filter risk: low. Plain text, one link, no spam-trigger words ("free," "guarantee," urgency), no ALL CAPS or exclamation stacking, single question-mark subject, no tracking pixel or image, and a real reply-oriented CTA rather than a hard sell — all of which keep it out of aggressive spam scoring and (mostly) out of Promotions. Subject carries {{company}} so it renders unique per recipient (good). Honest caveats the original understated: (1) Legal/compliance is the real exposure, not content. A cold commercial send in the US should carry a CAN-SPAM footer — valid physical mailing address + a working opt-out — or be genuinely 1:1 and low-volume; EU/UK recipients fall under GDPR/PECR (legitimate-interest basis + easy opt-out; some markets like Germany effectively require prior consent), so segment those out or tread carefully. (2) Reputation risk is medium, not low: useanswr.com is a new, unwarmed domain, and a brand-new URL in the body can drag deliverability — warm the domain first, authenticate SPF/DKIM/DMARC, send from a warmed subdomain (never the root), keep the first cold batch small, and consider dropping the link entirely on first touch (ask for the reply, send the link only to those who bite). Net: low content risk, medium overall risk driven by compliance footer + cold-domain reputation.
